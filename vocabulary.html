A small glossary

Astrology & tarot, defined.

The vocabulary we use most often, written for someone who has never opened a book on astrology. If a term you are looking for isn't here, please bring it to the intro call — we love these questions.

Approximately fifty terms · A to Z

A B C D E F H I J L M N O P R S T V W Z

A

  • Air signs

    The element associated with intellect, communication, and ideas. Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius. People with strong air placements often live in their thinking.

  • Aspect

    A geometric relationship between two planets in a chart — measured by the angle between them. Aspects are how the planets "speak" to each other. Common aspects include the conjunction, sextile, square, trine, and opposition.

  • Ascendant (or Rising sign)

    The zodiac sign that was rising over the eastern horizon at the moment of your birth. It is calculated from your birth time and place. The Ascendant shapes how you appear to others and how you meet the world. Without an exact birth time, the Ascendant cannot be calculated precisely.

  • Astrocartography

    A specialty of astrology that maps your chart onto the geography of the Earth — showing where on the planet certain planetary energies are strongest for you. Useful for considering moves, travel, or relocation.

B

  • Birth chart (or Natal chart)

    A map of the sky at the exact moment and place of your birth, showing where every planet was and the relationships between them. Your birth chart is the foundation of every reading we offer. It belongs to no one but you.

C

  • Cardinal signs

    Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn. The signs that mark the beginning of each season. People with strong cardinal placements tend to be initiators — they start things.

  • Chart rectification

    A technique for estimating a birth time when it is unknown, by working backward from significant life events. It is patient, painstaking work — not always exact, but often enough to give us useful angles.

  • Conjunction

    An aspect of zero degrees — two planets sitting together in the same place. Their energies blend and amplify each other. The most powerful aspect.

  • Cups (tarot)

    One of the four suits of the Minor Arcana. Associated with water, emotion, intuition, and relationships. When many cups appear in a reading, the question is usually a matter of the heart.

D

  • Degree

    Each zodiac sign covers thirty degrees of the sky. A planet's exact position is described by sign and degree (e.g. "Venus at 14° Taurus"). Degrees matter for precise aspect calculations.

  • Descendant

    The point opposite the Ascendant — what was setting in the west at your birth. It speaks to relationships, partnerships, and what we are drawn toward in others.

E

  • Earth signs

    Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn. The element of the material, the practical, the grounded. Strong earth placements often bring steadiness and a love of the tangible.

  • Eclipse

    When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align so that one shadows another. Solar eclipses (new moon) often coincide with sudden beginnings or revelations; lunar eclipses (full moon) often with culminations or endings. Eclipses are considered powerful and somewhat unpredictable.

  • Ephemeris

    A reference table showing where each planet is in the sky on any given day. The traditional astrologer's almanac. Modern software has largely replaced the paper version, but the word remains.

F

  • Fire signs

    Aries, Leo, Sagittarius. The element of energy, action, and passion. Strong fire placements often bring warmth, initiative, and the willingness to take a chance.

  • Fixed signs

    Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius. The signs that hold the middle of each season — and tend, in people, to hold their convictions. Persistence is a fixed gift.

H

  • House

    The chart is divided into twelve houses, each representing an area of life — the first house is the self, the seventh is partnership, the tenth is career and public life, and so on. The houses are calculated from your exact birth time, which is why time matters.

  • House systems

    Different mathematical methods for dividing the chart into houses. Placidus, Whole Sign, Koch, Equal — astrologers have preferences. We work with several and will explain which we have used in your reading.

I

  • IC (Imum Coeli)

    "The bottom of the sky" — the lowest point of the chart, opposite the Midheaven. It speaks to roots, home, ancestry, and the private self. What you return to when no one is watching.

J

  • Jupiter

    The planet of expansion, generosity, wisdom, and good fortune. Where Jupiter sits in your chart tends to be a place of natural abundance — and sometimes of overconfidence.

L

  • Lunar return

    A chart drawn for the moment each month when the Moon returns to the exact position it occupied at your birth. Some astrologers read it as a forecast for the coming lunar month.

  • Luminaries

    The Sun and the Moon. Technically not planets, but treated as the two most important bodies in any chart. They represent, broadly, the conscious self (Sun) and the inner emotional self (Moon).

M

  • Major Arcana

    The twenty-two named, numbered cards of the tarot — The Fool, The Magician, The High Priestess, and so on. They speak to the deeper, more archetypal themes of a question.

  • Mars

    The planet of action, drive, anger, and desire. Where Mars sits in your chart shows how you pursue what you want — and how you handle conflict.

  • Mercury

    The planet of communication, thought, and exchange. Mercury describes how you take in information and how you express yourself.

  • Mercury retrograde

    A period (three times a year, three weeks each) when Mercury appears to move backward from Earth's perspective. Often associated with miscommunication, technology hiccups, and the wisdom of pausing. Famously over-blamed, but worth noting.

  • Midheaven (MC)

    "The top of the sky" — the highest point of your chart. It speaks to public life, career, reputation, and what you become known for. Calculated from your birth time and place.

  • Minor Arcana

    The remaining fifty-six cards of the tarot, divided into four suits (cups, wands, swords, pentacles) numbered ace through ten plus four court cards. They speak to the practical and immediate.

  • Moon

    In your chart, the Moon represents your inner emotional self — the part of you that is not always shown to others. Where the Moon sits often describes what you need to feel safe and held.

  • Mutable signs

    Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces. The signs that close each season — and tend toward flexibility, adaptation, and change.

N

  • Neptune

    The planet of dreams, imagination, intuition, and illusion. Where Neptune sits in your chart is where the boundary between self and world is thinnest — for better and for worse.

  • Nodes (Lunar Nodes)

    Two points in the chart — the North Node and the South Node — that mark where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic. Often read as indicators of soul-level direction: what you are growing toward (North) and what you are letting go of (South).

O

  • Opposition

    An aspect of 180° — two planets directly across the chart from each other. Often experienced as tension or a polarity that needs to be reconciled.

  • Orb

    The margin of allowance for an aspect to still be considered "in effect." A conjunction at 0° is exact; one within a few degrees still counts. Different astrologers use different orbs.

P

  • Pentacles (tarot)

    One of the four tarot suits. Associated with earth — money, work, body, the material world. When many pentacles appear, the question is usually about resources or the tangible.

  • Pluto

    The planet (still, in astrology) of transformation, power, the underworld, and what cannot be avoided. Pluto's transits are slow, deep, and life-changing.

  • Progressions

    A technique for showing how a chart matures over time. Each day after birth corresponds, symbolically, to one year of life. Useful for spotting major internal shifts.

R

  • Retrograde

    When a planet appears, from Earth's perspective, to move backward in the sky. All planets except the Sun and Moon go retrograde periodically. Retrograde periods often coincide with internal review, redoing, or reconsidering what that planet governs.

  • Rising sign

    See Ascendant.

S

  • Saturn

    The planet of structure, discipline, time, and consequence. Saturn's lessons are not always comfortable but they are usually necessary. Saturn returns (around ages 29-30 and 58-60) are well-known milestones.

  • Sextile

    An aspect of 60° — a gentle, supportive harmony between two planets. Easy energy, but it requires some effort to use.

  • Sidereal astrology

    An alternative zodiac system used in Vedic and some Western astrology, based on the actual position of constellations rather than the seasonal tropical zodiac most Western astrologers use. Different starting point; different sign placements.

  • Spread (tarot)

    The pattern in which tarot cards are laid out for a reading. Different spreads suit different questions — three-card spreads for quick reflections, the Celtic Cross for fuller pictures.

  • Square

    An aspect of 90° — a tense, friction-generating relationship between two planets. Squares are the chart's growing edges. Difficult, often productive.

  • Sun sign

    The zodiac sign the Sun was in when you were born. The "sign" most people know about themselves. It is one of many factors in your chart — never the whole picture.

  • Swords (tarot)

    One of the four tarot suits. Associated with air — thought, communication, conflict, clarity. When many swords appear, the question often involves the mind or a difficult truth.

T

  • Tarot

    A deck of seventy-eight cards used for reflection and divination. Twenty-two Major Arcana and fifty-six Minor Arcana. We read tarot as a present-moment language — what is alive for you right now.

  • Transit

    The current movement of planets in the sky, considered in relation to your birth chart. "Transit reading" looks at what is happening above now, and how it touches the chart you were born with. Transits are the basis of timing in astrology.

  • Trine

    An aspect of 120° — the most harmonious aspect. Two planets in the same element, supporting each other. Trines are gifts; they can also be lazy, because the energy flows so easily we may not develop it.

V

  • Venus

    The planet of love, beauty, value, and pleasure. Where Venus sits in your chart describes what and whom you find beautiful — and how you express affection.

  • Void of course Moon

    A period when the Moon is making no more major aspects before it changes signs. Traditionally considered a poor time to start something new — the action will, the old astrologers said, "come to nothing."

W

  • Wands (tarot)

    One of the four tarot suits. Associated with fire — energy, creativity, action, drive. When many wands appear, the question often involves movement or new beginnings.

  • Water signs

    Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces. The element of emotion, intuition, and depth. Strong water placements often feel deeply and read others well.

Z

  • Zodiac

    The band of sky through which the Sun, Moon, and planets appear to move — divided into twelve signs of thirty degrees each. The familiar names — Aries, Taurus, Gemini, and so on — name the signs, not the constellations directly behind them (in tropical astrology).
